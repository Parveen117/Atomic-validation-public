# Claim Scope

## Demonstrated in the current internal validation

The V4 method constructs two local estimates of nuclear binding energy per nucleon:

1. a neutron-direction reconstruction at fixed proton number; and
2. a proton-direction reconstruction at fixed neutron number.

The estimates are combined using uncertainty-aware weights. A prospective guard abstains when no eligible direction exists, for selected ultralight nuclei, or when the two directional estimates disagree excessively.

On the current AME/NUBASE-derived working dataset, the guarded response retained 3,514 predictions from 3,558 evaluated nuclei and achieved a mean absolute residual of 10.2147 keV/A.

## Supported empirical interpretation

The present evidence supports the interpretation that agreement between independently constructed directional estimates is a useful reliability signal for local nuclear binding-energy reconstruction.

The evidence also supports explicit abstention as preferable to forcing predictions in identifiable high-risk regimes.

## Not yet demonstrated

The current validation does not by itself establish:

- prediction of measurements released after the model and guards were frozen;
- superiority over all established nuclear mass models;
- unbiased extrapolation far beyond measured regions;
- a fundamental derivation of nuclear structure;
- uniqueness of the two-axis reconstruction;
- universality across all nuclear observables;
- confirmation of any broader philosophical or foundational framework.

## Publication language

### Preferred

- guarded two-axis reconstruction;
- local nuclear binding-energy response;
- uncertainty-weighted directional blend;
- explicit abstention;
- chain-excluded validation;
- internally calibrated empirical result;
- reproducible computational validation.

### Avoid unless separately demonstrated

- exact universal atomic law;
- complete replacement for nuclear theory;
- proof of a foundational physical framework;
- parameter-free prediction, unless every fitted or calibrated quantity is fully removed;
- external validation, until a genuinely external dataset or later measurement release is used.

## Evidence hierarchy

The publication should keep three layers separate:

1. **Result:** measured performance of the frozen algorithm under a stated validation protocol.
2. **Interpretation:** directional agreement acts as a practical reliability signal.
3. **Hypothesis:** possible connection to a broader mathematical or physical framework.

A reader must be able to accept the first layer without being required to accept the third.
